\begin{abstract}
\noindent
Research on 4chan's /pol/ board routinely frames its information environment as
a contest between mainstream and alternative news media: legacy outlets are
rejected, a partisan counter-canon takes their place. We test that framing on
24 daily captures spanning 45 days (2026-07-22 to 2026-09-04): 6{,}532 threads,
278{,}706 posts, 10{,}078 citation events over 1{,}408 unique domains, each
carrying a transformer sentiment score for the surrounding discussion, and each
domain coded against a manually built, published codebook.

The framing fails, but not in the way a null result usually fails. The
mainstream--alternative contrast is flat: $p_{\text{Holm}}=1.0$, Cliff's
$\delta=-0.045$. Crucially, the effect \emph{shrank} as statistical power
grew---manual recoding raised the alternative category from 69 to 185 citations
and moved $\delta$ from $-0.121$ to $-0.045$---which is the signature of a real
null rather than an underpowered test. The three news categories
(mainstream, alternative, state-controlled) are statistically indistinguishable
from one another ($H=2.97$, $p=0.23$).

What separates cleanly is journalism from everything else. Citations to news
outlets of any kind sit in markedly more negative discussion than citations to
platforms, archives, institutional references, or the unclassified remainder:
$-0.345$ against $-0.183$, $p=3.6\times10^{-54}$, $\delta=-0.267$, with 66.1\%
against 41.3\% of citations in negative-scoring posts. /pol/'s hostility is
organised around journalism as an activity, not around a political axis running
through it. The alternative outlets supposed to constitute a counter-canon are
received exactly as coldly as the BBC.

We also report that news media occupy 12.8\% of citations---platforms take
43.7\%, archives and file hosts 11.0\%---that sourcing is extremely concentrated
(Gini $0.821$; YouTube alone 28.9\%), and that 0.68\% of posts (95\% CI
[0.44, 1.07]) disappear between repeated captures of the same thread, a measured
floor on the survivorship bias that conditions all such corpora.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

4chan's /pol/ (``Politically Incorrect'') board occupies an outsized place in
accounts of online radicalisation and information disorder. Measurement work has
characterised its language, its raiding behaviour, and its position in the wider
web ecosystem \citep{hine2017,papasavva2020}. A recurring assumption in this
literature, and in journalism drawing on it, is that /pol/ constitutes an
\emph{alternative media ecosystem}: a space where legacy outlets are rejected and
replaced by a counter-canon of partisan publishers.

The assumption is rarely measured directly. Doing so requires knowing what /pol/
cites, in what proportion, and how the surrounding discussion treats each kind of
source. This paper supplies that measurement for a bounded collection window, and
reports that the assumption is wrong in a specific and informative way: the axis
along which /pol/ sorts sources is not partisan, it is occupational.

Our contributions:

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{A well-powered null} on the mainstream--alternative contrast,
strengthened rather than weakened by added statistical power.
\item \textbf{A positive finding that replaces it}: news citations of every
political stripe attract more hostile discussion than non-news citations, with
the three news categories statistically homogeneous.
\item \textbf{A published codebook} of 203 manually coded domains, and evidence
that automated domain lists built from US-centric rating databases undercount
news on an internationally-sourced corpus by a factor of 2.8.
\item \textbf{A measured bound on survivorship bias}, from repeated captures of
the same threads, in place of the usual qualitative caveat.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Related work}

\citet{hine2017} gave the first large-scale measurement of /pol/, characterising
posting dynamics, hate-speech prevalence, and raids on other platforms.
\citet{papasavva2020} released a 3.5-year corpus of augmented /pol/ posts,
establishing dataset conventions much subsequent work relies on. Both establish
/pol/ as an object of quantitative study; neither takes the citation event as
the unit of analysis.

Source-classification work in computational social science generally anchors
labels to third-party rating databases rather than author judgment---AllSides,
Media Bias/Fact Check, and Ad Fontes are common choices
\citep{bozarth2020,robertson2018}. Section~\ref{sec:coding} reports what happens
when that approach meets a corpus whose news citations are substantially
non-American.

For sentiment we use the TweetEval RoBERTa model
\citep{barbieri2020,loureiro2022}, whose limitations on this domain we treat as
a first-order threat (Section~\ref{sec:limits}).

\section{Data and methods}

daily cadence, giving 24 daily captures between 2026-07-22 and 2026-09-04. This
is a \emph{snapshot} design: conclusions are scoped to the collection window,
not to /pol/ in general or across time.

\subsection{Citation extraction}

The unit of analysis is the \textbf{citation event}: one external domain
appearing in one post, deduplicated within the post. Links internal to 4chan are
excluded. Mobile and shortened hosts are normalised to a canonical domain
(\texttt{youtu.be}, \texttt{m.youtube.com} $\rightarrow$ \texttt{youtube.com}),
and subdomain matching resolves cases such as \texttt{en.wikipedia.org}.

We flag one failure mode because it is a plausible silent defect in any such
pipeline. An earlier version of ours retained only the first domain per post and
discarded the rest. Because /pol/ posts characteristically lead with a video or
tweet and append a source, this systematically discarded the appended source:
alternative-media citations were undercounted 3.6-fold. All figures here use
per-citation counting.

\subsection{Source coding and the undercount it corrected}
\label{sec:coding}

Domains are coded on a medium-type axis into seven categories: mainstream,
alternative, and state-controlled news; social platform; institutional
reference; archive/file host; and unclassified. Deterministic structural rules
handle platforms, \texttt{.gov}/\texttt{.mil}/\texttt{.edu} suffixes, and the
Wikimedia family.

An initial pass using curated news lists derived from US-centric rating
databases left 47.2\% of citations unclassified and put news outlets at 4.6\% of
citations. Inspection showed that figure was an artefact. The unclassified
bucket contained \texttt{dailymail.com}, \texttt{abc.net.au},
\texttt{independent.co.uk}, \texttt{itv.com}, \texttt{standard.co.uk},
\texttt{telegraph.co.uk}, \texttt{timesofisrael.com}, \texttt{aljazeera.com},
\texttt{pravda.com.ua} and many others---British, Australian, Israeli and
Ukrainian outlets absent from lists built around the American media market,
on a corpus dominated by threads about ongoing wars.

We therefore hand-coded the 200 most-cited unclassified domains, covering 74.9\%
of that bucket, and published the result as a versioned codebook of 203 domains.
Four coding rules were fixed in advance and applied uniformly:

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Aggregators} (\texttt{msn.com}, \texttt{yahoo.com}) are coded by
what they redistribute; search engines and link shorteners are not news.
\item \textbf{State-controlled} is defined by ownership and funding, not
editorial independence. This places Al Jazeera, the BBC, ABC (Australia), PBS,
NHK, Deutsche Welle, Anadolu, Channel News Asia and Minnesota Public Radio
together, and moved the BBC (135 citations) out of mainstream.
\item \textbf{Opinion Substacks and blogs} count as alternative media: they
produce editorial content.
\item \textbf{Campaign sites} are unclassified rather than a new category; state
media outlets remain state-controlled.
\end{enumerate}

Manual coding raised news media from 4.6\% to \textbf{12.8\%} of citations---a
2.8-fold undercount by the automated pass---and cut the unclassified bucket from
47.2\% to 23.6\%. Section~\ref{sec:limits} states what this coding does and does
not license, and Section~\ref{sec:robust} shows why the paper's central finding
does not depend on its most contestable choice.

\subsection{Sentiment measurement}

Each post carrying a citation is scored with
\texttt{cardiffnlp/twitter-roberta-base-sentiment-latest}
\citep{barbieri2020,loureiro2022}; the three-way softmax collapses to a compound
score $s = p_{\text{pos}} - p_{\text{neg}}$ in $[-1,+1]$. A post is scored once
and the score attached to each of its citations.

The score is computed over the \emph{entire post}. It measures the
\textbf{ambient tone of discussion surrounding a citation}, not the author's
stance toward the cited source. ``CNN is garbage, here's proof'' scores negative
and attaches to CNN even though the poster endorses the link's content. Every
tone result below must be read under that construct definition.

\subsection{Statistical analysis}

Proportions carry 95\% Wilson score intervals \citep{wilson1927}. Concentration
is measured by Gini and a normalised Herfindahl--Hirschman index over per-domain
citation counts. Tone uses Kruskal--Wallis \citep{kruskal1952} as omnibus with
$\varepsilon^2$, then Dunn post-hoc tests with tie correction and Holm step-down
adjustment \citep{holm1979}; pairwise effect sizes are Cliff's $\delta$
\citep{cliff1993} at the thresholds of \citet{romano2006}. The adjusted model
regresses compound sentiment on category, thread topic, and
$\log(1+\text{post length})$, with standard errors clustered by thread. We
report effect sizes throughout: at $n=10{,}078$, significance is cheap.

\subsection{Concentration}

The diet is narrow. The domain distribution has Gini $0.821$ and normalised HHI
$0.091$. YouTube alone supplies \textbf{28.9\%} of citations; the top ten supply
49.1\% and the top fifty 69.0\%. Twelve domains account for half of all
citations, while 989 of 1{,}408 domains (70.2\%) appear exactly once
(Figure~\ref{fig:lorenz}).

\subsection{The mainstream--alternative contrast is null}

Tone differs across categories overall (Kruskal--Wallis $H=278.0$,
$p=4.2\times10^{-57}$), at $\varepsilon^2=0.028$; the adjusted model reaches
$R^2=0.073$.

The contrast the literature's framing predicts is absent. Mainstream against
alternative gives $z=-1.03$, $p_{\text{Holm}}=1.0$, $\delta=-0.045$. The
adjusted model agrees: relative to platforms, mainstream carries $-0.104$
($p=4.8\times10^{-9}$) and alternative $-0.055$ ($p=0.075$), and their
difference is not distinguishable.

The strength of this null rests on how it behaved under added power. Before
manual recoding, alternative held 69 citations and $\delta$ was $-0.121$; that
result was properly reported as underpowered. Recoding raised alternative to 185
citations---a 2.7-fold increase---and $\delta$ fell to $-0.045$. An effect that
\emph{shrinks} as $n$ grows is not an effect awaiting detection.

\subsection{What separates instead: journalism from everything else}

The three news categories are statistically homogeneous: Kruskal--Wallis
restricted to mainstream, alternative and state-controlled gives $H=2.97$,
$p=0.23$. None of their three pairwise contrasts survives correction
(all $p_{\text{Holm}}\geq 0.88$).

\subsection{Robustness to the contestable coding choice}
\label{sec:robust}

Our most consequential coding decision---defining state-controlled by ownership
rather than editorial independence, which moves the BBC out of mainstream---is
defensible but arguable. It does not threaten the central finding. Because the
three news categories are statistically indistinguishable, any reallocation
\emph{among} them leaves the news/non-news partition, and therefore
Table~\ref{tab:newsblock}, unchanged. The finding is insensitive to precisely
the boundary a reviewer is most likely to contest.

\subsection{Archives and OSINT trackers}

Two behaviours emerge that a news-centric coding scheme would hide. Archives and
file hosts (\texttt{files.catbox.moe}, \texttt{rentry.org},
\texttt{archive.4plebs.org}, \texttt{archive.today}) take 11.0\% of citations,
more than any single news category: hosting media outside platform moderation
and preserving content, including 4chan's own deleted threads, against removal.

Within the remaining unclassified bucket, 571 citations (5.7\% of the corpus,
24.0\% of that bucket) across 14 domains are live war-mapping and vehicle
tracking services---\texttt{liveuamap}, \texttt{deepstatemap.live},
\texttt{marinetraffic}, \texttt{flightradar24}, \texttt{adsbexchange}. On a
board whose largest thread topic is armed conflict, primary-source OSINT tooling
is cited about half as often as all news media combined.

\subsection{Co-citation structure}

Domains co-occurring within a thread form a weighted graph (edges of weight
$\geq 2$): 286 nodes, 1{,}846 edges, density $0.045$, Louvain modularity
$0.429$ over six communities. The largest (198 domains) is general-purpose,
anchored on YouTube, Wikipedia and mainstream outlets. Smaller ones are
topical---a war-tracking cluster, a US-politics platform cluster
(\texttt{twitter}, \texttt{truthsocial}, \texttt{rumble}), a German-language
cluster. Sourcing ecosystems organise by topic and platform affordance, not by
editorial orientation---consistent with the main result.

\subsection{A measured bound on survivorship}
\label{sec:deletion}

Sixty-two pairs of repeated captures of the same thread let us measure what is
usually asserted: \textbf{19 of 2{,}776 observed posts (0.68\%, 95\% CI
[0.44, 1.07])} had disappeared by the following capture, affecting 7 threads.

This must be read for what it is. It measures post-level deletion \emph{within
threads that survived to be captured twice}. It says nothing about threads
deleted entirely, nor about threads born and dying between two daily
captures---which, at a daily cadence on a board with /pol/'s velocity, is the
larger loss channel. It is a \textbf{floor on content loss}, from a
self-selected sample, not an estimate of it. We report it because a measured
floor is more useful than an unmeasured caveat.

\section{Discussion}

\paragraph{The axis is occupational, not partisan.}
/pol/ does not sort sources into trusted counter-media and distrusted legacy
media. It sorts them into journalism and not-journalism, and treats the first
with uniform coldness. Breitbart-style outlets, the supposed counter-canon,
attract the same reception as the BBC; state broadcasters attract the same
reception as both. Whatever generates hostility, it is not tracking the
partisan position of the outlet.

Two readings are consistent with this. Under the first, the hostility is toward
professional gatekeeping as such---an anti-institutional disposition that does
not discriminate between institutions. Under the second, it is not about outlets
at all: news links are the ones that carry contested political events, and the
tone belongs to the event rather than the source. Our construct
(Section~\ref{sec:limits}) cannot separate these, and the adjusted model, which
controls only for coarse thread topic, does not settle it. We regard
disentangling them as the most valuable next step, and stance detection
\citep{mohammad2016,pontiki2016} as the instrument for it.

\paragraph{The frame is also mis-scaled.}
A literature asking whether /pol/ prefers alternative to mainstream media is
asking about 10.1\% of what /pol/ circulates, and about a distinction the board
does not appear to draw. Platforms supply four times as many citations as all
news combined; archives alone outnumber any single news category. Treating this
as a \emph{news} ecosystem imports a category the object does not honour.

\paragraph{Significance is not magnitude.}
Category explains under 3\% of sentiment variance ($\varepsilon^2=0.028$;
adjusted $R^2=0.073$). Even the news/non-news split, the strongest contrast we
find, is a small effect by conventional thresholds. Post length predicts tone
about as reliably as category does. These are real regularities in a noisy
process, not determinants of it.

\paragraph{A cautionary note on automated source lists.}
Our first pass, using curated lists derived from US-centric rating databases,
put news at 4.6\% of citations. Hand-coding put it at 12.8\%. The error was not
random: it removed British, Australian, Israeli and Ukrainian outlets from a
corpus whose dominant subject is war outside the United States. Any audit
applying American media-bias taxonomies to an internationally-sourced corpus
should expect an undercount of this order and check for it directly.

\section{Limitations}
\label{sec:limits}

\paragraph{Sentiment measurement is unvalidated on this domain.}
The model was trained on Twitter; /pol/'s irony, slang and toxicity are
out-of-distribution, and we have \emph{not} performed the human-annotated
validation that would quantify the resulting measurement error. All tone results
should be treated as provisional pending a hand-coded sample with reported
model--human agreement. The descriptive, concentration and coding results do not
depend on the sentiment model.

\paragraph{The codebook is single-coder.}
The 203 domains were coded by one author against fixed written rules, with
uncertain cases flagged (12 of 203). No second coder annotated independently, so
no Cohen's $\kappa$ or Krippendorff's $\alpha$ is reported. The structural
categories---platform, archive, institutional---rest on deterministic rules and
are robust; the news subdivisions rest on that single-coder judgment. The
central finding pools those subdivisions and is correspondingly less exposed
(Section~\ref{sec:robust}).

\paragraph{Coding is single-axis.}
A fuller design codes each domain on three orthogonal axes---medium type,
editorial orientation, ownership---against external databases (AllSides, MBFC,
Ad Fontes, RSF), since forcing them into one label is what makes Fox News, BBC
and France 24 irreconcilable. We code medium type only, with ownership folded
into the state-controlled category by rule.

\paragraph{A quarter of citations remain unclassified.}
23.6\% of citations fall outside all categories, concentrated in a long tail:
904 of the residual domains are cited exactly once. Hand-coding covered 74.9\%
of the original unclassified bucket; the remainder is reported as-is and could
contain further news outlets, which would raise the 12.8\% figure again without
affecting the news/non-news contrast's direction.

\paragraph{Survivorship bias is bounded below, not corrected.}
See Section~\ref{sec:deletion}. Moderation removes the most extreme content
preferentially, so the surviving corpus is filtered in the direction of the
phenomenon measured. Source-distribution estimates are relatively robust, since
deletion would need to correlate with cited-source category. Tone estimates are
directly exposed.

\paragraph{Topic classification is weak.}
Thread topics come from keyword matching over the opening post's subject and
slug, and 76.2\% of posts fall into ``Other / Misc''. The topic covariate is a
coarse control, not a topical analysis---which matters for the second reading in
the Discussion, since a better topic control is what would test it.

\paragraph{Scope.}
One board, 24 daily captures, 45 days. Results describe the surviving, publicly
visible discourse of /pol/ during that window. They do not generalise across
time or to other imageboards.

\section{Conclusion}

The question the field asks of /pol/---does it prefer alternative media to
mainstream media?---has an answer, and the answer is that the board does not
appear to make the distinction. Mainstream, alternative and state-controlled
citations attract statistically indistinguishable hostility, and the null
strengthened when we tripled the power available to detect a difference. What
does separate is journalism from everything else: news citations of any kind sit
in markedly more negative discussion than platform, archive, institutional or
unclassified ones.

That reframing has a methodological consequence and a substantive one. The
methodological one is that source taxonomies built for the American media market
misdescribe an internationally-sourced corpus badly enough to invert a headline
figure. The substantive one is that studying /pol/'s ``media diet'' along a
left--right axis measures a distinction the board does not use. The productive
next steps are target-dependent stance detection, to separate hostility toward
outlets from hostility toward events, and a topic model good enough to make that
separation testable.
