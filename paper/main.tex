\begin{abstract}
\noindent
4chan's /pol/ is routinely described as an \emph{alternative media ecosystem}:
a space where legacy outlets are rejected and a partisan counter-canon takes
their place. The claim is widely repeated and rarely measured. We measure it
over 24 daily captures spanning 45 days (2026-07-22 to 2026-09-04): 6{,}532
threads, 278{,}706 posts, and 10{,}078 citation events across 1{,}408 unique
domains, each domain coded against a published codebook.

Three findings work against the framing. First, news outlets of any kind
occupy \textbf{12.8\%} of citation events; social platforms take 43.7\% and
archives and file hosts 11.0\%---the latter cited more often than any single
news category. Second, sourcing is extremely concentrated: Gini $0.821$,
YouTube alone supplying 28.9\% of citations, twelve domains supplying half, and
70.2\% of domains appearing exactly once. Third, and most directly, there is no
alternative-media cluster in the co-citation structure. Within the news
subgraph, assortativity by orientation is $-0.042$; alternative outlets show no
tendency to co-occur with one another (1 observed edge against 0.81 expected
under a degree-preserving null, $p=0.62$) and are peripheral (mean degree $0.9$
against $2.2$ for mainstream). Communities partition by topic and platform
affordance, not by editorial orientation.

We report two methodological results alongside. Curated source lists derived
from US-centric rating databases undercounted news on this corpus by a factor
of 2.8, systematically dropping British, Australian, Israeli and Ukrainian
outlets from a corpus dominated by threads about wars outside the United
States. And repeated captures of the same threads put a measured floor on
survivorship bias: 0.68\% of posts (95\% CI [0.44, 1.07]) disappear between
observations.

We deliberately exclude sentiment analysis. A transformer scored every cited
post, but the measure could not be validated on this domain and we judged it
unfit to carry a conclusion; Section~\ref{sec:nosentiment} states the reasoning
in full.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

4chan's /pol/ (``Politically Incorrect'') board occupies an outsized place in
accounts of online radicalisation and information disorder. Measurement work has
characterised its language, its raiding behaviour, and its position in the wider
web ecosystem \citep{hine2017,papasavva2020}. A recurring assumption in that
literature, and in journalism drawing on it, is that /pol/ constitutes an
alternative media ecosystem, with a counter-canon of partisan publishers
displacing the legacy press.

Testing the claim requires knowing what /pol/ cites, in what proportion, and
whether its sources organise along a partisan axis. This paper supplies that
measurement for a bounded window. Our contributions:

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{A citation-level audit} of 10{,}078 citation events with
proportions as Wilson score intervals, showing news of any kind at 12.8\%.
\item \textbf{A concentration analysis} quantifying how narrow the diet is.
\item \textbf{A structural test} of the counter-canon claim: co-citation
assortativity by editorial orientation, which finds none.
\item \textbf{A published codebook} of 203 manually coded domains, and evidence
that US-centric source taxonomies undercount news 2.8-fold here.
\item \textbf{A measured floor on survivorship bias} from repeated captures.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Related work}

\citet{hine2017} gave the first large-scale measurement of /pol/, characterising
posting dynamics, hate-speech prevalence, and raids on other platforms.
\citet{papasavva2020} released a 3.5-year corpus establishing dataset
conventions much subsequent work relies on. Both establish /pol/ as an object of
quantitative study; neither takes the citation event as the unit of analysis.

Source-classification work in computational social science generally anchors
labels to third-party rating databases rather than author judgment---AllSides,
Media Bias/Fact Check and Ad Fontes are common choices
\citep{bozarth2020,robertson2018}. Section~\ref{sec:coding} reports what happens
when that approach meets a corpus whose news citations are substantially
non-American.

/pol/ in general or across time.

\subsection{Citation extraction}

The unit of analysis is the \textbf{citation event}: one external domain in one
post, deduplicated within the post. Links internal to 4chan are excluded.
Mobile and shortened hosts normalise to a canonical domain
(\texttt{youtu.be}, \texttt{m.youtube.com} $\rightarrow$ \texttt{youtube.com}),
and subdomain matching resolves cases such as \texttt{en.wikipedia.org}.

We flag one failure mode because it is a plausible silent defect in any such
pipeline. An earlier version of ours retained only the first domain per post.
Because /pol/ posts characteristically lead with a video or tweet and append a
source, this systematically discarded the appended source: alternative-media
citations were undercounted 3.6-fold. All figures here use per-citation counting.

\subsection{Source coding and the undercount it corrected}
\label{sec:coding}

Domains are coded on a medium-type axis into seven categories: mainstream,
alternative and state-controlled news; social platform; institutional reference;
archive/file host; and unclassified. Deterministic structural rules handle
platforms, \texttt{.gov}/\texttt{.mil}/\texttt{.edu} suffixes and the Wikimedia
family.

An initial pass using curated news lists derived from US-centric rating
databases left 47.2\% of citations unclassified and put news outlets at 4.6\%.
That figure was an artefact. The unclassified bucket contained
\texttt{dailymail.com}, \texttt{abc.net.au}, \texttt{independent.co.uk},
\texttt{itv.com}, \texttt{standard.co.uk}, \texttt{telegraph.co.uk},
\texttt{timesofisrael.com}, \texttt{aljazeera.com} and \texttt{pravda.com.ua}:
British, Australian, Israeli and Ukrainian outlets absent from lists built
around the American media market, on a corpus dominated by threads about
ongoing wars.

We therefore hand-coded the 200 most-cited unclassified domains, covering 74.9\%
of that bucket, and publish the result as a versioned codebook of 203 domains.
Four rules were fixed in advance and applied uniformly:

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Aggregators} (\texttt{msn.com}, \texttt{yahoo.com}) are coded by
what they redistribute; search engines and link shorteners are not news.
\item \textbf{State-controlled} is defined by ownership and funding, not
editorial independence. This groups Al Jazeera with the BBC, ABC (Australia),
PBS, NHK, Deutsche Welle, Anadolu, Channel News Asia and Minnesota Public Radio,
and moves the BBC (135 citations) out of mainstream.
\item \textbf{Opinion Substacks and blogs} count as alternative media.
\item \textbf{Campaign sites} stay unclassified; state media outlets remain
state-controlled.
\end{enumerate}

Manual coding raised news media from 4.6\% to \textbf{12.8\%} of citations---a
2.8-fold undercount by the automated pass---and cut the unclassified bucket from
47.2\% to 23.6\%.

\subsection{Why this paper contains no sentiment analysis}
\label{sec:nosentiment}

We scored every cited post with
\texttt{cardiffnlp/twitter-roberta-base-sentiment-latest}
\citep{barbieri2020,loureiro2022} and then excluded the results. The reasoning
is worth stating, because the measure is a standard one and its omission is
deliberate.

Three problems compound. \textbf{Construct}: the score is computed over the whole
post, so it captures the ambient tone of a message, not the author's stance
toward the source cited in it. Inspection of scored posts finds hostility
directed at reported events, at ethnic groups, and at other posters, all of it
attributed to whichever domain the post happened to link---including cases where
the source is cited approvingly as supporting evidence. \textbf{Domain shift}:
the model was trained on Twitter. /pol/'s irony reads literally (the most
positively-scored news post in the corpus is a sarcastic caption on a census
map, scored $+0.90$), and the slurs the board uses as ordinary register are read
as hostility regardless of the poster's actual attitude. \textbf{Truncation}:
scoring caps at 128 tokens, and 42.3\% of cited posts exceed it.

Any of these could be quantified with a hand-annotated validation sample, which
we have not produced. Publishing a headline conclusion on an uncalibrated
instrument seemed worse than publishing a narrower paper without one. The
scoring code remains in the repository behind a flag so the analysis can be
revisited after validation.

Everything reported below is derived from citation structure alone.

\subsection{Statistical analysis}

Proportions carry 95\% Wilson score intervals \citep{wilson1927}. Concentration
uses the Gini coefficient and a normalised Herfindahl--Hirschman index over
per-domain citation counts. The co-citation graph links domains appearing in the
same thread, keeping edges of weight $\geq 2$; communities come from Louvain,
and orientation sorting is measured by attribute assortativity
\citep{newman2003}. Because alternative domains are rarely cited and therefore
sparsely connected, the clustering test permutes labels only among nodes of
comparable degree---a naive label permutation would credit them with
connectivity they do not have.

News media account for 12.8\% of citation events; platforms for 43.7\%; archives

Proportions are stable across the window with no trend: over the 24 captures,

(median 43.3\%) and archive share 6.2--14.4\% (median 11.0\%).

\subsection{Concentration}

The diet is narrow. The domain distribution has Gini $0.821$ and normalised HHI
$0.091$. YouTube alone supplies \textbf{28.9\%} of citations; the top ten supply
49.1\% and the top fifty 69.0\%. Twelve domains account for half of all
citations, while 989 of 1{,}408 domains (70.2\%) appear exactly once
(Figure~\ref{fig:main}b).

\subsection{No alternative-media cluster}
\label{sec:network}

If /pol/ maintained a counter-canon, alternative outlets should travel together:
cited in the same threads as one another rather than alongside the established
press. They do not.

The co-citation graph has 286 nodes and 1{,}846 edges (density $0.045$), with
Louvain modularity $0.429$ over six communities. Assortativity by category over
the full graph is $+0.054$---domains co-occur with little regard to what kind of
source they are.

Restricting to news domains sharpens the test. The news subgraph has 100 nodes
(68 mainstream, 20 alternative, 12 state-controlled) and 97 edges. Assortativity
by orientation is $\mathbf{-0.042}$: if anything slightly disassortative, and
indistinguishable from no sorting. Alternative outlets share exactly one edge
with another alternative outlet, against $0.81$ expected under a
degree-preserving null ($p=0.62$). They are also peripheral: mean degree $0.9$,
against $2.2$ for mainstream and $2.1$ for state-controlled. Most alternative
citations appear in threads where no other news source is cited at all.

The communities that do exist are topical. The largest (198 domains) is
general-purpose, anchored on YouTube, Wikipedia, the BBC and the Guardian, and
holds 59 mainstream, 14 alternative and 10 state-controlled domains
\emph{together in the same community}. The smaller ones are a war-tracking
cluster (\texttt{marinetraffic}, \texttt{flightradar24}, \texttt{flightaware},
\texttt{oilprice}), a US-politics platform cluster (\texttt{twitter},
\texttt{truthsocial}, \texttt{rumble}, \texttt{tiktok}), and a file-sharing
cluster (\texttt{files.catbox.moe}, \texttt{t.me}, \texttt{archive.today}).
Sourcing organises by subject matter and platform affordance, not by editorial
orientation.

\subsection{Archives and OSINT trackers}

Two behaviours emerge that a news-centric scheme would hide. Archives and file
hosts (\texttt{files.catbox.moe}, \texttt{rentry.org},
\texttt{archive.4plebs.org}, \texttt{archive.today}) take 11.0\% of citations:
hosting media outside platform moderation, and preserving content---including
4chan's own deleted threads, via 4plebs---against removal.

Within the unclassified remainder, 571 citations (5.7\% of the corpus, 24.0\% of
that bucket) across 14 domains are live war-mapping and vehicle-tracking
services: \texttt{liveuamap}, \texttt{deepstatemap.live}, \texttt{marinetraffic},
\texttt{flightradar24}, \texttt{adsbexchange}. On a board whose largest
identifiable thread topic is armed conflict, primary-source OSINT tooling is
cited about half as often as all news media combined.

\subsection{A measured floor on survivorship}
\label{sec:deletion}

Sixty-two pairs of repeated captures of the same thread let us measure what is
usually asserted: \textbf{19 of 2{,}776 observed posts (0.68\%, 95\% CI
[0.44, 1.07])} had disappeared by the following capture, affecting 7 threads.

This must be read for what it is. It measures post-level deletion \emph{within
threads that survived to be captured twice}. It says nothing about threads
deleted entirely, nor about threads born and dying between two daily
captures---which, at a daily cadence on a board with /pol/'s velocity, is the
larger loss channel. It is a \textbf{floor on content loss} from a self-selected
sample, not an estimate of it. We report it because a measured floor is more
useful than an unmeasured caveat.

\section{Discussion}

\paragraph{The counter-canon is not there to be found.}
On this board, in this window, alternative outlets are neither numerous (1.8\% of
citations) nor connected to one another (one co-citation edge among twenty
domains) nor central (mean degree below one). They appear as isolated citations
inside threads organised around topics, next to the mainstream outlets they are
supposed to displace. Whatever /pol/'s relationship to the legacy press,
``replaced by an alternative ecosystem'' does not describe the citation
structure.

\paragraph{The frame is also mis-scaled.}
A literature asking whether /pol/ prefers alternative to mainstream media is
asking about 10.1\% of what /pol/ circulates. Platforms supply more than three
times as many citations as all news combined; archives alone outnumber any
single news category. The board's characteristic act is not passing along an
article but passing along a video, a screenshot, a mirror, or a map.

\paragraph{A cautionary note on automated source lists.}
Our first pass, using curated lists derived from US-centric rating databases, put
news at 4.6\% of citations. Hand-coding put it at 12.8\%. The error was not
random: it removed British, Australian, Israeli and Ukrainian outlets from a
corpus whose dominant subject is war outside the United States. Any audit
applying American media-bias taxonomies to an internationally-sourced corpus
should expect an undercount of this order and check for it directly.

\paragraph{What we cannot say.}
Nothing here speaks to how /pol/ \emph{treats} the sources it cites. Citation is
not endorsement, and on a board whose characteristic mode is derision it may
often be the opposite. Establishing that would require the stance measurement
this paper declines to fake (Section~\ref{sec:nosentiment}), and it remains the
obvious next study.

\section{Limitations}
\label{sec:limits}

\paragraph{The codebook is single-coder.}
The 203 domains were coded by one author against fixed written rules, with 12
uncertain cases flagged. No second coder annotated independently, so no Cohen's
$\kappa$ or Krippendorff's $\alpha$ is reported. Structural
categories---platform, archive, institutional---rest on deterministic rules and
are robust; the news subdivisions rest on that single judgment.

\paragraph{The network test is small.}
Twenty alternative domains and 97 news-subgraph edges is a thin basis. The
absence of clustering is consistent with the descriptive picture and with the
community structure, but a larger corpus could reveal structure this one cannot
resolve. We report it as evidence against the counter-canon claim, not as proof
of its impossibility.

\paragraph{Coding is single-axis.}
A fuller design codes each domain on three orthogonal axes---medium type,
editorial orientation, ownership---against external databases, since forcing
them into one label is what makes Fox News, BBC and France 24 irreconcilable. We
code medium type only, with ownership folded into the state-controlled category
by rule. Our orientation test inherits that coarseness: it asks whether
mainstream, alternative and state-controlled outlets cluster, not whether a
finer left--right gradient does.

\paragraph{A quarter of citations remain unclassified.}
23.6\% fall outside all categories, concentrated in a long tail of which 904
domains are cited exactly once. Hand-coding covered 74.9\% of the original
unclassified bucket; the remainder could contain further news outlets, which
would raise the 12.8\% figure again.

\paragraph{Survivorship bias is bounded below, not corrected.}
See Section~\ref{sec:deletion}. Moderation removes the most extreme content
preferentially, so the surviving corpus is filtered in the direction of the
phenomenon studied. Source-distribution estimates are relatively robust, since
deletion would need to correlate with cited-source category.

\paragraph{Scope.}
One board, 24 daily captures, 45 days. Results describe the surviving, publicly
visible discourse of /pol/ during that window, and do not generalise across time
or to other imageboards.

\section{Conclusion}

/pol/'s information diet is not a media diet. News outlets of every political
description together supply an eighth of its citations, while platforms supply
more than four in ten and archives more than one in ten. The alternative press
that the board is supposed to have adopted in place of the legacy press is
rare, peripheral, and unclustered: its outlets do not travel together, they
travel beside the mainstream outlets they were supposed to replace, inside
threads organised by subject matter.

Two methodological results follow the substantive one. Source taxonomies built
for the American media market misdescribe an internationally-sourced corpus
badly enough to invert a headline figure. And a measure that cannot be validated
on its target domain is better excluded than caveated---a narrower paper is
worth more than a broader one resting on an uncalibrated instrument. The
productive next step is the stance measurement this paper declines to attempt:
what /pol/ does with the sources it cites, as opposed to which ones it cites.
